Napier - Electorate Profile

  1. Home
  2. MPs and Electorates
  3. Electorate profiles 2020
  4. Napier - Electorate Profile
Originally published: 31 July 2020
Last updated: 27 April 2021

Electorate boundaries map

Enlarge image

Source: Parliamentary Library

The Napier electorate incorporates Napier city and its environs, including Bay View, Poraiti, Wairoa, Lake Waikaremoana and the Mahia Peninsula.  The electorate extends out along State Highway 5 towards Tarawera, with the north-eastern boundary extending almost to the outskirts of Gisborne and inland as far as Matawai.

The electorate was established for the 1861 election and has existed ever since. The first MP to hold the seat, Henry Stark, is also the MP with the shortest term representing the electorate. He served from 19 February to 27 April 1861 before resigning. Reverend William Colenso, a prominent missionary, printer, botanist, and explorer, won the seat in the resulting by-election and served until 1866. Napier was a two-member electorate from 1876 to 1881.

Boundary changes in 2014 saw the Napier electorate gain population from the East Coast electorate.  There have been no changes to the boundary for the 2020 election.

Another prominent early holder of the seat was Sir Donald McLean (1866 to 1877), who was at various times Chief Land Purchase Officer, Native Secretary, the Superintendent of Hawke’s Bay, the official in charge of military operations on the East Coast, and Native Minister. Since 1928 Napier has been predominantly held by Labour MPs, except for the periods 1951 to 1954 and 2005 to 2014, when National MPs held the seat. The longest-serving MP for Napier was Geoff Braybrooke (Labour) who served from 1981 to 2002. The Napier electorate is currently held by Stuart Nash (Labour), who has held the seat since 2014.

According to the 2018 Census, over three-quarters (77.7%) of the population belong to the European ethnic group; over one-quarter (27.1%) of the usually resident population is of Māori ethnicity, compared to 16.5% in New Zealand overall. Two-thirds (66.4%) of households are one-family households, while over one-quarter (26.9%) are one-person households, somewhat higher than in New Zealand overall (22.7%).

The share of those employed in the following industry sectors were all above the New Zealand average:

  • Manufacturing (12.3%)
  • Agriculture, forestry, and fishing (10.4%)
  • Health care and social assistance (9.8%)

The proportion of households earning income between $50,000 and $100,000 (31.3%) is slightly above the New Zealand average share (29.3%). About one third (32.6%) of households received NZ Superannuation or Veteran's Pension, above the New Zealand average (26.7%).

Election results (2020)

The winning candidate in the 2020 election, Stuart Nash (Labour Party), captured a majority (52.0%) of the 41,035 valid electorate votes cast for candidates in the Napier electorate, with a margin of 5,856. The seat was previously won in 2017 by Stuart Nash (Labour Party) with a margin of 5,220. The Labour Party also captured a majority (50.9%) of the party votes in Napier, up 13.1 percentage points on its party vote share in 2017. Turnout (total votes cast as a proportion of enrolled electors) in 2020 was 84.6%.

This section has a variety of information about people living in the electorate. Topics include population, ethnicity, religion, language, health, and iwi affiliation. The information comes from the 2018 Census, except for iwi affiliation. Information on iwi affiliation comes from the 2013 Census as this information is not available for 2018.

There is information on both Māori ethnicity and Māori descent as these are different concepts.  Māori descent is based on ancestry and Māori ethnicity is based on cultural affiliation.

This section provides a range of information from the 2018 Census about homes and families in the electorate. It also has information about residential building consents issued.

The census distinguishes between dwellings, households, and families. A dwelling refers to the physical structure. A household is either one person who usually lives alone, or two or more people who usually live together. A family comprises a couple with or without children or one parent and their children.

There are two tables describing homeownership. They differ in measuring ownership at the individual or the household level. A household may own their dwelling but not all people within that household share in the ownership.
