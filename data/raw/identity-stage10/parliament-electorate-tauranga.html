Tauranga - Electorate Profile

  1. Home
  2. MPs and Electorates
  3. Electorate profiles 2020
  4. Tauranga - Electorate Profile
Originally published: 3 August 2020
Last updated: 12 July 2022

Electorate boundaries map

Enlarge image

Source: Parliamentary Library

The Tauranga electorate comprises Tauranga city and its environs, bounded to the west by the Wairoa River and to the east by the Waiorohi Stream. It includes Mt Maunganui and Tauranga Airport.

The electorate has existed continuously since 1908, and had previously existed from 1881 to 1890. The first MP to be elected to the newly reformed electorate was William Herries (Independent, then Reform), who held the seat from 1908 until his death in 1923. Herries was a senior member of the Massey government and held the portfolios of Native Affairs and Railways, among others.

There were some changes to the southern boundary in 2014 to meet population quota requirements, which resulted in Poike and Waimapu being moved into the Bay of Plenty electorate. There have been no changes to the boundary for the 2020 election.

The longest serving MPs for Tauranga have been George Walsh (National) who held it for 21 years from 1951 to 1972, and Winston Peters (National, Independent, then New Zealand First) who held it from 1984 to 2005 (also 21 years). The Tauranga electorate was then held by Bob Clarkson (National) from 2005 to 2008, and then by Simon Bridges (National), who held the seat from 2008 until 2022. The electorate has been won by the Labour Party only once, when Charles Burnett held the seat from 1935 to 1938. The Tauranga electorate is currently represented by Sam Uffindell (National), who won the seat in the 2022 by-election.

At the 2018 census over a quarter (26.5%) of the electorate was aged over 60, compared to the national average of one-fifth (20.2%). Over one-third (34.2%) of households receive New Zealand superannuation or a veteran’s pension, the fourth-highest share among general electorates. The share of those over 15 whose highest qualification was a Level 6 diploma (6.5%) was the highest in the country.

Of those employed, the proportion working in the health care and social assistance industry (12.8%) was the most prevalent and the fourth-highest among the general electorates. The share of those working in the construction industry (10.5%) and retail trade (9.9%) were the second and third most common industries in the electorate. Over half (57.7%) of families earnt income above $70,000, slightly below the national average (60.3%).

Election results (2020)

The winning candidate in the 2020 election, Simon Bridges (National Party), captured a plurality (43.5%) of the 43,070 valid electorate votes cast for candidates in the Tauranga electorate, with a margin of 1,856. The seat was previously won in 2017 by Simon Bridges (National Party) with a margin of 11,252. The Labour Party captured a plurality (42.3%) of the party votes in Tauranga, up 15.5 percentage points on its party vote share in 2017. Turnout (total votes cast as a proportion of enrolled electors) in 2020 was 85.6%.

On Saturday 18 June 2022 a by-election was held for the Tauranga electorate following the resignation of Simon Bridges (National Party). The winning candidate, Sam Uffindell (National Party), captured a majority (55.9%) of the 20,765 valid electorate votes cast for candidates in the Tauranga by-election. His winning margin was 6,354. (In a by-election no party votes are cast). Turnout (total votes cast as a proportion of the 51,706 enrolled electors) in the 2022 by-election was 40.5%.

There is information on both Māori ethnicity and Māori descent as these are different concepts.  Māori descent is based on ancestry and Māori ethnicity is based on cultural affiliation.

This section provides a range of information from the 2018 Census about homes and families in the electorate. It also has information about residential building consents issued.

The census distinguishes between dwellings, households, and families. A dwelling refers to the physical structure. A household is either one person who usually lives alone, or two or more people who usually live together. A family comprises a couple with or without children or one parent and their children.

There are two tables describing homeownership. They differ in measuring ownership at the individual or the household level. A household may own their dwelling but not all people within that household share in the ownership.
