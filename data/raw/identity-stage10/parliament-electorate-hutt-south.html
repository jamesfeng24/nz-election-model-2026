Hutt South - Electorate Profile

  1. Home
  2. MPs and Electorates
  3. Electorate profiles 2020
  4. Hutt South - Electorate Profile
Originally published: 31 July 2020
Last updated: 27 April 2021

Electorate boundaries map

Enlarge image

Source: Parliamentary Library

Hutt South comprises most of the city of Lower Hutt including the eastern communities of Wainuiomata and Eastbourne, and the western hill suburbs of Korokoro, Maungaraki, Normandale and Belmont.

The Hutt South electorate was created ahead of the move to the MMP voting system in 1996. It merged the old electorates of Pencarrow and Eastern Hutt. 

In the 2014 boundary review, Hutt South lost most of Naenae and a small part of Epuni to the Rimutaka (now Remutaka) electorate, in exchange for the suburbs of Kelson and Belmont. It also gained the suburbs of Tirohanga, Harbour View, Normandale, Maungaraki and Korokoro from Ōhariu.  There have been no changes to the boundary for the 2020 election.

Hutt South was held by Trevor Mallard (Labour) from 1996 to 2017. In July 2016 Mallard announced he would not contest the seat at the 2017 election but would run as a list-only candidate with the intention of becoming the Speaker of the House. Following the 2017 election he did become Speaker. The Hutt South electorate was held by Chris Bishop (National), from 2017 to 2020. At the 2020 election it was won by Ginny Andersen (Labour).

The ethnic profile of Hutt South revealed at Census 2018 is similar to the New Zealand average: 71.3% are European (compared to 70.2% nationally); 16.2% are Maori (16.5%); 15.9% are Asian (15.1%), 8.1% are Pasifika (8.1%); and 1.3% identify with one or more of the Middle Eastern/Latin American/African ethnic grouping (1.5% nationally).

Although professionals (29.1%) and managers (17.2%) make up nearly half the Hutt South workforce, the third-largest grouping is clerical and administrative workers who comprise 13.3% of the workforce – the second-highest proportion in New Zealand. About one in seven (14.2%) mainly travelled to work by train, the second-largest share among general electorates.

Election results (2020)

The winning candidate in the 2020 election, Ginny Andersen (Labour Party), captured a plurality (49.99%) of the 44,908 valid electorate votes cast for candidates in the Hutt South electorate, with a margin of 3,777. The seat was previously won in 2017 by Chris Bishop (National Party) with a margin of 1,530. The Labour Party also captured a majority (55.1%) of the party votes in Hutt South, up 14.7 percentage points on its party vote share in 2017. Turnout (total votes cast as a proportion of enrolled electors) in 2020 was 86.3%.

This section has a variety of information about people living in the electorate. Topics include population, ethnicity, religion, language, health, and iwi affiliation. The information comes from the 2018 Census, except for iwi affiliation. Information on iwi affiliation comes from the 2013 Census as this information is not available for 2018.

There is information on both Māori ethnicity and Māori descent as these are different concepts.  Māori descent is based on ancestry and Māori ethnicity is based on cultural affiliation.

This section provides a range of information from the 2018 Census about homes and families in the electorate. It also has information about residential building consents issued.

The census distinguishes between dwellings, households, and families. A dwelling refers to the physical structure. A household is either one person who usually lives alone, or two or more people who usually live together. A family comprises a couple with or without children or one parent and their children.

There are two tables describing homeownership. They differ in measuring ownership at the individual or the household level. A household may own their dwelling but not all people within that household share in the ownership.
