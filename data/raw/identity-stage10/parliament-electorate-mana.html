Mana - Electorate Profile

  1. Home
  2. MPs and Electorates
  3. Electorate profiles 2020
  4. Mana - Electorate Profile
Originally published: 28 July 2020
Last updated: 27 April 2021

Electorate boundary map

Enlarge image

Source: Parliamentary Library

The Mana electorate is situated in the lower North Island, north of Wellington. It comprises the northern parts of the Wellington suburb of Tawa, the Porirua Harbour communities of Porirua City, Titahi Bay, Whitby, Mana, and Plimmerton, and the Kapiti Coast communities of Pukerua Bay, Paekākāriki, Raumati South, and Raumati Beach. 

The area has long been populated and there were Māori settlements at Wainui and Whareroa up until the mid-1800s. The electorate contains Queen Elizabeth Park, which was opened during the 1953 Royal Visit.  During World War II the area hosted 20,000 US Army and Marine troops in three main camps in and around what is now Queen Elizabeth Park.

The Mana electorate was created in 1996 for the introduction of MMP. It took in parts of the discontinued Porirua electorate as well the southern part of the discontinued Kapiti electorate, including Paraparaumu south of the airport and Raumati.  Slight boundary changes to Mana occurred in 2007 which saw parts of Paraparaumu, east of State Highway 1, also included in the electorate.  There have been no changes to the boundary for the 2020 election.

Previous MPs to have held the seat are Graham Kelly (Labour) from 1996 to 2003, and Luamanuvao Winifred Alexandra Laban (Labour) from 2003 to 2010.  Laban was the first female Pacific Island MP to hold an electorate seat.  Laban resigned as an MP on 10 August 2010, resulting in a by-election the same year. Kris Faafoi (Labour) won the by-election and held the seat until the 2020 election. He was the first MP of Tokelauan descent to hold a parliamentary electorate. Barbara Edmonds (Labour) won the seat at the 2020 election.

Among the general electorates, Mana has the fifth-highest proportion of Pacific Peoples (21.1%), almost triple the national average (8.1%). One fifth (20.0%) of the population are Māori and one-quarter (25.1%) of the population were born overseas.

With a long-standing and electrified rail network from Wellington to Porirua and the Kapiti Coast, the Mana electorate ranks first in New Zealand for the proportion of people (15.2%) who travel to work by train, over seven times the New Zealand average.

Mana has the fifth-highest proportion of those working in the public administration and safety area (12.3%). The share of those working in the professional, scientific and technical services fields (11.0%), the construction industry (10.7%), and the health care and social assistance fields (10.6%) are all above the national averages.

Election results (2020)

The winning candidate in the 2020 election, Barbara Edmonds (Labour Party), captured a majority (62.0%) of the 42,136 valid electorate votes cast for candidates in the Mana electorate, with a margin of 16,244. The seat was previously won in 2017 by Kris Faafoi (Labour Party) with a margin of 10,980. The Labour Party also captured a majority (58.5%) of the party votes in Mana, up 13.0 percentage points on its party vote share in 2017. Turnout (total votes cast as a proportion of enrolled electors) in 2020 was 84.3%.

This section has a variety of information about people living in the electorate. Topics include population, ethnicity, religion, language, health, and iwi affiliation. The information comes from the 2018 Census, except for iwi affiliation. Information on iwi affiliation comes from the 2013 Census as this information is not available for 2018.

There is information on both Māori ethnicity and Māori descent as these are different concepts.  Māori descent is based on ancestry and Māori ethnicity is based on cultural affiliation.

This section provides a range of information from the 2018 Census about homes and families in the electorate. It also has information about residential building consents issued.

The census distinguishes between dwellings, households, and families. A dwelling refers to the physical structure. A household is either one person who usually lives alone, or two or more people who usually live together. A family comprises a couple with or without children or one parent and their children.

There are two tables describing homeownership. They differ in measuring ownership at the individual or the household level. A household may own their dwelling but not all people within that household share in the ownership.
