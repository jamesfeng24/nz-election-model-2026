Whanganui - Electorate Profile

  1. Home
  2. MPs and Electorates
  3. Electorate profiles 2020
  4. Whanganui - Electorate Profile
Originally published: 6 August 2020
Last updated: 27 April 2021

Electorate boundaries map

Enlarge image

Source: Parliamentary Library

The Whanganui electorate includes the communities of Whanganui, Hāwera, Eltham, Stratford, and Pātea. The Whanganui River flows through the electorate, and a large part of the Whanganui National Park falls within it.

The electorate has been in existence from 1860. Its precursor, Wanganui and Rangitīkei electorate, was one of the original electorates from 1853. The name of the electorate was changed from Wanganui to Whanganui ahead of the 1996 election.

There were no changes to Whanganui’s boundaries in the 2020 boundary review.

Three Whanganui MPs were Premier (Prime Minister) of the colony of New Zealand: William Fox, Julius Vogel, and John Ballance. Labour held the seat from 1935 until 2005, apart from two terms. The longest-serving MP for the electorate was Joe Cotterill (Labour) who held it for eight terms. Another longstanding MP for Whanganui was Russell Marshall (Labour), a Cabinet Minister in the 4th Labour government, who held the seat for six terms. Jill Pettis (Labour) was the first woman to win the seat, holding it from 1993 to 2005. From 2005 until 2020 Whanganui was held by National, most recently by Harete Hipango, who became the first Māori MP for Whanganui when she won it in 2017. At the 2020 general election the electorate was won by Steph Lewis (Labour).

The Whanganui electorate is predominantly European (80.7%) and Māori (25.4%). The proportion of people identifying with the Asian ethnic group (3.8%) is about one-quarter the New Zealand average, while the share of the Pacific Peoples ethnic group (2.9%) is about one-third the 8.1% share for New Zealand as a whole. The share of those born overseas (11.1%) was the second-lowest across general electorates.

In 2018 it had the highest shares both of those who had some difficulty seeing, even if wearing glasses (20.3%), and who had some difficulty washing all over or dressing (5.3%). Whanganui had the second-highest share among general electorates of those on a supported living payment (2.7%), and those who derived their income from ACC or a private work accident insurer (2.2%). Well over one-quarter (27.3%) of those over 15 had no qualification, the highest share in New Zealand.

The manufacturing industry employed almost one-fifth (18.4%) of those employed – the highest share among general electorates. About one third worked in one of three industries: agriculture, forestry, and fishing (12.2%); health care and social assistance (11.3%); retail trade (8.8%). Almost one-fifth (17.8%) of families earnt income between $50,000 and $70,000, the second-highest share across New Zealand.

Election results (2020)

The winning candidate in the 2020 election, Stephanie Lewis (Labour Party), captured a majority (55.7%) of the 40,010 valid electorate votes cast for candidates in the Whanganui electorate, with a margin of 8,191. The seat was previously won in 2017 by Harete Hipango (National Party) with a margin of 1,706. The Labour Party also captured a majority (51.7%) of the party votes in Whanganui, up 15.1 percentage points on its party vote share in 2017. Turnout (total votes cast as a proportion of enrolled electors) in 2020 was 82.9%.

This section has a variety of information about people living in the electorate. Topics include population, ethnicity, religion, language, health, and iwi affiliation. The information comes from the 2018 Census, except for iwi affiliation. Information on iwi affiliation comes from the 2013 Census as this information is not available for 2018.

There is information on both Māori ethnicity and Māori descent as these are different concepts.  Māori descent is based on ancestry and Māori ethnicity is based on cultural affiliation.

This section provides a range of information from the 2018 Census about homes and families in the electorate. It also has information about residential building consents issued.

The census distinguishes between dwellings, households, and families. A dwelling refers to the physical structure. A household is either one person who usually lives alone, or two or more people who usually live together. A family comprises a couple with or without children or one parent and their children.

There are two tables describing homeownership. They differ in measuring ownership at the individual or the household level. A household may own their dwelling but not all people within that household share in the ownership.
