Botany - Electorate Profile

  1. Home
  2. MPs and Electorates
  3. Electorate profiles 2020
  4. Botany - Electorate Profile
Originally published: 30 July 2020
Last updated: 27 April 2021

Electorate boundary map

Enlarge image

Source: Parliamentary Library

The Botany electorate extends from Cockle Bay in the north, down through Botany Downs and East Tāmaki, to Flat Bush in the south. Its western border, south of Burswood, extends to the Tāmaki River.

Botany Downs is one of Auckland’s newest suburbs, having been developed from the late 1990s by the Manukau City Council and private enterprise.  The Botany electorate was established for the 2008 election following the 2006 census, which showed high population growth in the Auckland region.

Boundary changes in 2014 saw more of the Flat Bush area west of Chapel Road incorporated into the electorate.  There have been no changes to the boundary for the 2020 election.

The Botany electorate was first held from 2008 to 2011 by Pansy Wong (National Party), who was New Zealand’s first Asian MP when elected as a list MP in 1996, and also New Zealand's first Asian Cabinet Minister when given the portfolios of Ethnic Affairs and Women's Affairs in 2008.  Wong resigned as an MP on 17 January 2011, resulting in a by-election the same year. Jami-Lee Ross, standing as a candidate for the National Party, won the by-election and represented the electorate until 2020. Ross resigned from the National Party in October 2018 and became an independent MP. The electorate was won in the 2020 general election by Christopher Luxon (National Party).

The 2018 Census shows a majority (53.9%) of the Botany electorate was born overseas, the third-highest proportion of any general electorate. The Asian ethnic group is the most prevalent in the electorate (44.9%), followed by Europeans (38.6%), and Pacific Peoples (13.7%). Over one in ten people (10.8%) don’t speak English, the highest proportion among general electorates.

Botany also ranked number one among general electorates for the highest proportion of: people affiliated with Buddhism (3.4%); those who were married (58.2%); couples with children (53.2%); those with an overseas secondary qualification (13.6%); households with four usual residents (22.4%); households with three cars (18.8%); and those working in the wholesale trade industry (8.8%).

Election results (2020)

The winning candidate in the 2020 election, Christopher Luxon (National Party), captured a majority (52.5%) of the 36,253 valid electorate votes cast for candidates in the Botany electorate, with a margin of 3,999. The seat was previously won in 2017 by Jami-Lee Ross (National Party) with a margin of 12,839. The Labour Party captured a plurality (47.6%) of the party votes in Botany, up 18.3 percentage points on its party vote share in 2017. Turnout (total votes cast as a proportion of enrolled electors) in 2020 was 74.0%.

This section has a variety of information about people living in the electorate. Topics include population, ethnicity, religion, language, health, and iwi affiliation. The information comes from the 2018 Census, except for iwi affiliation. Information on iwi affiliation comes from the 2013 Census as this information is not available for 2018.

There is information on both Māori ethnicity and Māori descent as these are different concepts.  Māori descent is based on ancestry and Māori ethnicity is based on cultural affiliation.

This section provides a range of information from the 2018 Census about homes and families in the electorate. It also has information about residential building consents issued.

The census distinguishes between dwellings, households, and families. A dwelling refers to the physical structure. A household is either one person who usually lives alone, or two or more people who usually live together. A family comprises a couple with or without children or one parent and their children.

There are two tables describing homeownership. They differ in measuring ownership at the individual or the household level. A household may own their dwelling but not all people within that household share in the ownership.
