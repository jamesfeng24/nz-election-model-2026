Rongotai - Electorate Profile

  1. Home
  2. MPs and Electorates
  3. Electorate profiles 2020
  4. Rongotai - Electorate Profile
Originally published: 6 August 2020
Last updated: 27 April 2021

Electorate boundaries map

Enlarge image

Source: Parliamentary Library

 The Rongotai electorate comprises the eastern communities of Wellington City including Roseneath, Hataitai, Melrose, Kilbirnie, Rongotai, Miramar and Seatoun. The western boundary runs south from the Basin Reserve taking in Newtown, Berhampore, Vogeltown, and Kingston, as well as the south coast suburbs of Ōwhiro Bay, Island Bay, Houghton Bay and Lyall Bay. The electorate also includes the Chatham Islands. Located within this electorate are Wellington Airport and Wellington Hospital.

Rongotai was a new electorate created for the first MMP election in 1996. Rongotai combined most of the former Island Bay and Miramar electorates, both of which were established in 1946.

No changes were made to the Rongotai electorate boundaries in the 2014 or 2020 Boundary Reviews.

Rongotai has always been held by Labour. Annette King was the member for Miramar in 1993 and subsequently won Rongotai in 1996, holding it until her retirement in 2017. King also served as MP for Horowhenua from 1984 to 1990, making her New Zealand’s longest-serving woman MP. King has served as Deputy Leader of the Labour Party and as Minister of Health, Police, Transport and Justice. Since 2017, Rongotai has been held by Paul Eagle.

Almost one-half (48.7%) of the Rongotai electorate was aged between 20 and 50 years in 2018, somewhat above the New Zealand average (40.4%). Almost half (47.9%) of the population over 15 had never married and been in a civil union, the fourth-highest share across New Zealand. The electorate had both the second-highest percentage of people from the combined Middle Eastern/Latin American/African ethnic group (4.4%), and those who had a post-graduate or honours degree (11.5%), double the New Zealand average (5.7%).

Of any general electorate in 2018, Rongotai had the highest percentage: for those renting, having the local authority as landlord (9.8%); of those who travelled to work by bus (23.3%); of those households with only one motor vehicle (46.5%); of those working in the information, media and telecommunications industries (5.6%); of those working in the arts and recreation sector (3.3%).

Election results (2020)

The winning candidate in the 2020 election, Paul Eagle (Labour Party), captured a majority (58.1%) of the 44,619 valid electorate votes cast for candidates in the Rongotai electorate, with a margin of 19,207. The seat was previously won in 2017 by Paul Eagle (Labour Party) with a margin of 10,900. The Labour Party also captured a majority (52.6%) of the party votes in Rongotai, up 8.2 percentage points on its party vote share in 2017. Turnout (total votes cast as a proportion of enrolled electors) in 2020 was 87.6%.

This section has a variety of information about people living in the electorate. Topics include population, ethnicity, religion, language, health, and iwi affiliation. The information comes from the 2018 Census, except for iwi affiliation. Information on iwi affiliation comes from the 2013 Census as this information is not available for 2018.

There is information on both Māori ethnicity and Māori descent as these are different concepts.  Māori descent is based on ancestry and Māori ethnicity is based on cultural affiliation.

This section provides a range of information from the 2018 Census about homes and families in the electorate. It also has information about residential building consents issued.

The census distinguishes between dwellings, households, and families. A dwelling refers to the physical structure. A household is either one person who usually lives alone, or two or more people who usually live together. A family comprises a couple with or without children or one parent and their children.

There are two tables describing homeownership. They differ in measuring ownership at the individual or the household level. A household may own their dwelling but not all people within that household share in the ownership.
