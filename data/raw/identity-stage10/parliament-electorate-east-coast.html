East Coast - Electorate Profile

  1. Home
  2. MPs and Electorates
  3. Electorate profiles 2020
  4. East Coast - Electorate Profile
Originally published: 30 July 2020
Last updated: 27 April 2021

Electorate boundary map

Enlarge image

Source: Parliamentary Library

The East Coast electorate is a mostly rural electorate, with a large land area incorporating the entire East Cape. Provincial population centres include Te Puke in the west, Whakatāne and Ōpōtiki on its northern boundary, and Gisborne in the east.  Other communities in the electorate are Maketu, Kawerau, Murupara, Minginui and Edgecumbe.

The electorate first existed from 1871 to 1893, when it was abolished. East Coast was recreated for the 1999 election from the Mahia electorate and parts of the Bay of Plenty electorate.

Boundary changes in 2014 saw some population areas south of Gisborne move into the Napier electorate while Murupara, Kawerau, and most of Te Urewera National Park were transferred into East Coast from Rotorua. There were no changes to East Coast electorate boundary for the 2020 election. 

Women have won East Coast in every election since it was re-established in 1999. Janet Mackey (Labour) held the seat from 1999 to 2005.  Anne Tolley (National) first won the seat in 2005 and held it until her retirement at the 2020 election. Kiri Allan (Labour) won the seat in 2020.

The East Coast electorate has the largest land area of any North Island general electorate at 13,814 square kilometres. A majority (51.5%) of the population are from the Māori ethnic group, the highest share among all 65 general electorates.

At the 2018 census, East Coast had the highest ranking (largest proportion) amongst the general electorates of:

  • Those born in New Zealand (89.3%)
  • Māori language speakers (17.4%)
  • Females with four children (10.7%)
  • Those who are regular smokers (22.1%)
  • Those on Jobseeker Support (12.3%)
  • Those who looked after a child not from their own household (19.1%)
  • Those who looked after an ill or disabled non-household member (11.4%)

Almost one in five (19.4%) of those employed were labourers, with 17.8% employed as professionals and a further 17.3% employed as managers. The most prevalent industry was agriculture, forestry and fishing (17.0%), followed by health care and social assistance (10.7%) and those in the manufacturing industry (10.4%).

Election results (2020)

The winning candidate in the 2020 election, Kiri Allan (Labour Party), captured a majority (51.7%) of the 41,431 valid electorate votes cast for candidates in the East Coast electorate, with a margin of 6,331. The seat was previously won in 2017 by Anne Tolley (National Party) with a margin of 4,807. The Labour Party also captured a majority (50.4%) of the party votes in East Coast, up 13.6 percentage points on its party vote share in 2017. Turnout (total votes cast as a proportion of enrolled electors) in 2020 was 81.5%.

This section has a variety of information about people living in the electorate. Topics include population, ethnicity, religion, language, health, and iwi affiliation. The information comes from the 2018 Census, except for iwi affiliation. Information on iwi affiliation comes from the 2013 Census as this information is not available for 2018.

There is information on both Māori ethnicity and Māori descent as these are different concepts.  Māori descent is based on ancestry and Māori ethnicity is based on cultural affiliation.

This section provides a range of information from the 2018 Census about homes and families in the electorate. It also has information about residential building consents issued.

The census distinguishes between dwellings, households, and families. A dwelling refers to the physical structure. A household is either one person who usually lives alone, or two or more people who usually live together. A family comprises a couple with or without children or one parent and their children.

There are two tables describing homeownership. They differ in measuring ownership at the individual or the household level. A household may own their dwelling but not all people within that household share in the ownership.
