Epsom - Electorate Profile

  1. Home
  2. MPs and Electorates
  3. Electorate profiles 2020
  4. Epsom - Electorate Profile
Originally published: 4 August 2020
Last updated: 27 April 2021

Electorate boundaries map

Enlarge image

Source: Parliamentary Library

The Epsom electorate comprises the Auckland city communities of Parnell, Newmarket, Remuera, Epsom, and Mt. Eden, with its western boundary running north along Dominion Rd from Balmoral up to Eden Terrace.

Epsom was created for the first MMP election in 1996. It replaced the electorates of Eden and Remuera.  At just under 20 square kilometres, it is the smallest electorate in New Zealand.

Boundary changes in 2014 saw Grafton and the Auckland Domain transferred out of the electorate and into Auckland Central.  There have been no changes to the boundary for the 2020 election.

The first MP, and the only woman to date, to represent Epsom was Christine Fletcher (National) who held the seat from 1996 to 1999. She had formerly been the MP for Eden from 1990 to 1996.  In 1998 she was elected Mayor of Auckland City and subsequently retired from Parliament at the 1999 election.  ACT New Zealand party leaders have held the seat since 2005: Rodney Hide (2005 to 2011), John Banks (2011 to 2014), and David Seymour, who has held the seat since 2014.

Epsom is a wealthy electorate of middle-aged professionals. At the 2018 Census Epsom had the highest proportion among general electorates of those who earnt over $150,000 per year (40.8%), and for those aged 50-54 (7.8%). Epsom also ranked first (had the highest proportions) on the following measures at the 2018 Census:

  • People who work in the professional, scientific and technical services industry (20.6%)
  • People who work in the rental, hiring or real-estate industry (4.0%)
  • People who have Bachelor's degrees as their highest qualification (27.7%)
  • People who have never smoked regularly (78.9%);
  • Houses that are owned through a trust (20.8%)

 

Election results (2020)

The winning candidate in the 2020 election, David Seymour (ACT New Zealand), captured a plurality (47.5%) of the 41,024 valid electorate votes cast for candidates in the Epsom electorate, with a margin of 9,224. The seat was previously won in 2017 by David Seymour (ACT New Zealand) with a margin of 5,519. The National Party captured a plurality (37.5%) of the party votes in Epsom, down -21.2 percentage points on its party vote share in 2017. Turnout (total votes cast as a proportion of enrolled electors) in 2020 was 82.8%.

This section has a variety of information about people living in the electorate. Topics include population, ethnicity, religion, language, health, and iwi affiliation. The information comes from the 2018 Census, except for iwi affiliation. Information on iwi affiliation comes from the 2013 Census as this information is not available for 2018.

There is information on both Māori ethnicity and Māori descent as these are different concepts.  Māori descent is based on ancestry and Māori ethnicity is based on cultural affiliation.

This section provides a range of information from the 2018 Census about homes and families in the electorate. It also has information about residential building consents issued.

The census distinguishes between dwellings, households, and families. A dwelling refers to the physical structure. A household is either one person who usually lives alone, or two or more people who usually live together. A family comprises a couple with or without children or one parent and their children.

There are two tables describing homeownership. They differ in measuring ownership at the individual or the household level. A household may own their dwelling but not all people within that household share in the ownership.
