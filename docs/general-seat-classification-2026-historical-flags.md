# Past exceptional flags by year (appendix to the 2026 classification) (Stage67 audit, 2014-2023) and the evidence behind each

**Important:** the repository records *which* seats were flagged (`data/processed/exceptional-balance-scale/design-contract.json`) but **not the reason given for each**. The columns below are the facts the Stage56 labelling template pre-fills from repository evidence (candidate change, boundary change, scandal tags) and are my reading of what probably drove each flag, not recorded reasons. `Source` shows whether the seat was also on the residual-ranked list shown to the auditor (`list`, biased: chosen because the model had missed it) or flagged without it (`blind`).

## What the facts say across all four elections

- **Candidate change (60 seat-elections):** 17 flagged (28%), 43 not flagged. A plain candidate change was therefore usually ordinary in the fit, which matches James's rule.
- **Boundary change (75 seat-elections):** 12 flagged (16%).
- **Scandal tag (6 seat-elections):** 5 flagged (Clutha-Southland 2017; Botany, Rangitata, Southland 2020; Hamilton West 2023).
- **No candidate, boundary or scandal fact at all:** 12 of the 38 flags (Pakuranga 2014; Epsom 2017, 2020, 2023; Ōhāriu 2017; Mt Albert 2020; Auckland Central, Botany, Ilam, Mt Roskill, Remutaka and Tāmaki 2023). Five are minor-party or deal seats (Epsom, Ōhāriu, Auckland Central). Mt Albert 2020, Botany 2023 and Remutaka 2023 were the seats of Ardern, Luxon and Hipkins, so party-leader profile is a plausible reason (my inference). The rest are likely residual-driven; 9 of these 12 were on the residual list.
