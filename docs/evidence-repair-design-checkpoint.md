## Frozen, outcome-independent identity evidence frame

**Universe and unit.** The frame is all 213 complete seat-transition clusters in the accepted observed/unchanged-boundary 2008→2011 (2007 regime), 2014→2017 (2014) and 2020→2023 (2020) comparisons: 63/64/65 general and seven Māori seats each time. Each cluster contains *every* election-local source and target candidate occurrence, including winners, losers, same-name candidates, possible replacements, entrants, independents and cancelled nominations. The source is the immutable Stage 7 occurrence export, which preserves original election-local IDs and labels; only year, scope, source electorate number/name, boundary regime, occurrence ID and held/cancelled status enter frame construction. Election-local seat number and the independently validated regime establish geography; source/target labels are checked only for spelling compatibility, including macrons. A shared name or seat label alone proves neither geography nor person identity.

**Fixed bounded sample.** Within each transition/scope stratum rank seat numbers by ascending SHA-256 of `nz-election-model-2026-evidence-repair-v1|sourceYear|targetYear|scope|two-digit seat number`, breaking a hash tie by seat number. Select the first eight general and first two Māori **whole seat clusters** per transition. This is 30 of 213 clusters, 415 occurrences (220 source, 195 target): general 115/116/130 and Māori 16/18/20 across the three transitions. All selected contests happen to be held; cancellation is retained in the frame and would not trigger replacement sampling. The exact 213-row frame, occurrence IDs, input hashes, selection flags and counts are saved in `data/processed/checkpoints/evidence-repair/cohort-inventory.json`. The sample is fixed before acquisition or effects. It does not rank on election outcomes, residuals, identity confidence, Parliament-profile availability or Stage 10's interesting cases. All 3,007 historical occurrences are already indexed in the source export; the proposed evidence workload is the selected 415, not a claim that all are identifiable.

**Uniform later evidence protocol (not executed here).** For each selected occurrence, in an order fixed by a separate hash of its occurrence ID, start with its official election-local candidature/nomination record. Seek occurrence-specific dated official candidate lists, party selection/nomination notices or equivalent authoritative records; compare with independently documented aliases or service dates when necessary. Name similarity, party, seat or a Parliament profile proving only that someone exists cannot confirm an occurrence. Use the same source hierarchy and search limit for source/target, winners/losers, continuations/replacements. Evidence may establish a person, this occurrence's link, a same/different-person relation and career intervals at different confidence levels; store these separately. A dated official result can establish an observed winning occurrence retrospectively, but target/later victory cannot decide acquisition order, link confirmation or evaluation eligibility. Distinguish historical fact date, publication date, retrieval date and unknown timing. A retrospectively retrieved source may prove a pre-result fact, while a source only created because of later success cannot establish balanced prospective coverage.

**Adjudication states and stopping.** Direct occurrence corroboration yields `confirmed`; an exact-chain projection supported by a separately corroborated person but lacking occurrence-specific evidence remains `probable`; conflicting, homonymous or inadequate evidence stays `unresolved`. Same-person and distinct-person relations require auditable evidence beyond strings; aliases, party/seat changes and return after absence remain unresolved until corroborated. Prior list service is separate from electorate tenure, by-election entry is dated, an interrupted former member is not a freshman, and first panel appearance is not first service. Unknown career history stays unknown even for confirmed occurrence identity. The proposed next pass stops after **two bounded authoritative searches per unresolved occurrence and at most 60 newly registered unique resources across the fixed sample**, with no replacement of unsolved clusters or winner-based top-ups. Preserve unresolved reason codes (`no_occurrence_source`, `ambiguous_alias_or_homonym`, `conflicting_sources`, `publication_timing_unknown`, `career_history_incomplete`, `budget_exhausted`) and the exact search/coverage ledger. This is a proposed acquisition budget, not authorization to retrieve any page in this checkpoint.

**Time interpretation.** The frame is outcome-independent computationally; the tests flip winner/residual fields without changing it. That does not prove future acquisition is balanced. Record all attempted searches, including failures, so source availability can be compared across outcome groups *after* selection. Identity reconstruction from retrospectively published sources is a retrospective analysis unless the evidence was demonstrably available by the target forecast cutoff. Use the official target nomination-close date as the common historical cutoff, to be source-pinned before later backtesting; no exact cutoff date is asserted here. Known target candidature may be conditioned on at that cutoff. A later backtest must report a strict as-of subset and a separate retrospective reconstruction; unknown publication timing cannot silently enter the former.

## Complete candidate-forecast designs for later testing

These are **design candidates**, not selected operational models. Common target is each named candidate's valid candidate votes and share of valid candidate votes, together with accounting of informal candidate, party-vote-only and candidate-only ballots. All votes/destinations in a contest must share one ballot-mass ledger; no per-candidate clipping or independent renormalization may create votes. A forecast needs either a pre-cutoff turnout/valid-ballot forecast with uncertainty or an explicitly conditional observed-turnout diagnostic. Observed target candidate totals, split cells, national support and denominators may score a holdout only; observed party vote may be an input solely to a labelled conditional component diagnostic.

| Later view | Prediction-time inputs and national support | Candidate vote mapping, difficult cases and overlap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Whole-ballot split allocation** | Source local/pooled split intervals, as-of candidature/party continuity, party-ballot and turnout **forecasts** or a declared national-support **scenario**; an observed target local party vote creates a conditional diagnostic only. | Allocate each forecast party-ballot group across *all* standing candidates plus informal-candidate/party-only destinations via a training-only, mass-conserving routing rule. Source local cells are used where applicable; entrants, absent destinations, independents, changed parties, replacements and unmatched rows require separately validated pooled fallback or an explicit unknown-mass interval/abstention. Never transfer a source candidate-specific cell to a replacement by default. Valid candidate votes are the sum of valid candidate destinations, including candidate-only contributions from informal-party rows where evidenced, **not** the party-vote denominator. Rounded rows and uncertain turnout propagate to vote/share intervals. This view contains its own candidate allocation; no Stage 7–10 or Stage 6 bonus is added. |
| **Party-response / candidate-baseline view** | Target-boundary source candidate baseline or defensible uncertainty range, source/target local party shares from an as-of national-support scenario/forecast and retained Stage 5 transforms; a separately forecast valid-candidate denominator. | Later review may test Stage 6-like local-party response conditional on baseline and candidate status, with β=1/simple carry-forward as benchmarks. Same-boundary historical seats support diagnostic tests; changed-boundary 2026 seats do not yet have candidate baselines. Unknown incoming history is a missing/broad prior, not zero; replacement does not inherit outgoing strength automatically. If a status-conditioned residual term is jointly fitted, it replaces rather than adds overlapping persistence, freshman or replacement terms. Preserve independent candidate-vote totals and coherent ballot constraints. |

Simple common-sample benchmarks for both are a party-only candidate prediction with a training-only candidate/party offset, prior candidate share/residual **only where identity and geography support it**, and source-year pooled split allocation where every ballot group can be accounted for. A benchmark can abstain too; report its abstentions beside the view. The Stage 11 omitted probable-continuation carry-forward remains a **deferred sensitivity**, not a Stage 11 result or operational assumption. A party-only offset or pooled routing parameter must be learned from earlier elections only, never the held target.

Before any comparison, freeze the same 2011, 2017 and 2023 unchanged-boundary target contest frame and official nomination-close cutoff. Use earlier-election training only, identical candidate/contest/ballot targets for errors, and separate general/Māori reporting. Report MAE, RMSE, bias, coverage, interval width, abstention and error on both the intersection and full eligible frame; include replacement/entrant/loser and party strata without quietly dropping them. A coverage-constrained score should make abstention visible. A conditional observed-party-vote experiment diagnoses candidate allocation; a complete forecast requires pre-cutoff party support, turnout, nomination, ballot-validity and denominator inputs. With three election clusters, do not call candidate-level iid uncertainty election-level replication. No ensemble weights or integration are selected here.

## Two remaining prerequisites

**Conditional Stage 6 review (separate authorization).** On a predeclared common NAT/LAB unchanged-boundary frame, fit a parsimonious conditional response to local party movement with candidate-turnover/status information available before target results, comparing with the original unconditional β and β=1/β=0 on identical chronological holdouts. Use Stage 5 additive/proportional/log-odds transforms in one reusable pipeline, retain their unresolved selection, and apply D027's materiality stopping rule: only continue precision work if the difference could change the operational decision relative to dominant candidate-baseline/identity uncertainty. Candidate status is unknown where not corroborated; do not encode unknown as continuation or use Stage 10's descriptive γ as a fixed correction. Recheck out-of-fold errors and coverage by transition; no refit is performed now. Any view still requires a target-boundary candidate baseline.

**2026 target-boundary candidate baseline (separate authorization).** The preserved 2023→2026 geography has a validated 2025-regime crosswalk, source controls and synthetic **party-vote** bounds for 64 general targets; it does not identify candidate-specific vote placement or transported residuals. The secondary-availability audit supplies predecessor population-origin coverage, not a candidate-ballot allocation. A later baseline would need validated source candidate ballots by source geography, candidate/party and status mapping onto 2026 nominations, a defensible geographic allocation or partial-identification rule for each predecessor, valid-candidate-vote denominator/turnout assumptions, and explicit uncertainty across feasible boundary weights and candidate transfer. Preserve Māori separately and Port Waikato missingness. Do not match changed seats by name, select independent marginal interval endpoints as one joint assignment, or attach a Stage 7 premium to synthetic party votes. This checkpoint constructs no baseline.
