# Stage 11 historical split-ticket modelling

## Evidence checkpoint — before fitting

Stage 10 PR #17 is merged as `8ae94ccc`; Stage 11 starts from that merged main. `evidence.json`, `applicability.json` and a snapshot of the 384 consumed official local split sources were produced before a Stage 11 statistical specification or fit. No historical numerical file or raw source has been changed.

Each local split row is a party-ballot group with an exact published count, including **Informal Party Votes**. Its columns are named candidate destinations, Informal Candidate Votes and Party Vote Only. Cell entries are two-decimal **percentages conditional on that party-ballot row**; local joint counts are not published. A source row count times the reported percentage is a rounded-cell approximation, not an observed count. Rows and columns are joined election-locally to independently published party and candidate totals. The total party-ballot denominator includes informal party ballots and differs from valid candidate votes, so split percentages cannot be silently substituted for published candidate shares. Candidate-only ballots are represented within the informal-party row where reported, not assigned to a valid-party group. A forecast using only valid-party votes would leave that contribution unresolved.

The 21 preserved aggregate reconciliation failures involve Party Vote Only and related row/column checks, not 21 independent errors. National exact split/non-split summaries do not reveal exact local joint cells. General/Māori/national **aggregate** split matrices group destinations by party rather than named local candidate; they cannot substitute for the missing Māori local candidate panel. The pre-fit applicability inventory covers every target candidature in adjacent elections. It requires the already validated unchanged-boundary transitions (2008→2011, 2014→2017, 2020→2023), same named seat, held contest and Stage5 party continuity. It records exact-chain name evidence as **probable**, never confirmed person identity or proof that a different name is a replacement. No target votes or winner flags enter eligibility. On those three transitions, 247/285/271 general candidates have a source candidate of the same continuing party and a comparable source local matrix. **Zero** candidates have every target party-ballot group represented in that source local matrix: party entry/exit creates unmatched row mass. Thus naive complete transport of local conditional percentages is not a defensible prediction of published target candidate totals. A bounded model must retain that mass or use a separately justified training-only fallback. Changed-boundary transitions, Māori scope, candidates without supported party continuity, cancelled contests, missing source candidacies and unresolved identity remain explicit in `applicability.json`.

The 2014 Internet MANA, 2020 Advance NZ/NZ Public Party and 2023 Freedoms NZ report groupings are source-local aggregate controls, not cross-election party-continuity permissions. Historical TOP is not a 2026 Opportunity prior. Split tables describe joint ballot **aggregates**; they do not identify an individual's voting transition or personal vote. Later modelling must distinguish conditional target-party-vote evaluation from an actual forecast using predicted party votes.

**Next checkpoint:** freeze a parsimonious prediction design and its benchmarks against these recorded coverage limits before calculating any fitted split parameters or holdout errors.

## Frozen statistical specification — before scoring

`specification.json` freezes a **matched-component conditional** task after the evidence checkpoint `6939dbd`. Complete local transport is unavailable because every comparable target contest contains unmatched party-vote groups. A model that asserted exact total candidate votes from the matched component would silently set the unknown contribution to zero. The primary calculation transports the source-seat, source-party-row percentage allocated to the source candidate's party to a target candidate of that continuing party. This tests party-level transfer, including cases where the named candidate changes; it does not identify a personal effect. Source percentages are treated as rounding intervals, with no pseudocounts or fit hyperparameters.

The two primary benchmarks use the same candidate-event and matched rows: a party-only diagonal allocation, and the source election's general-electorate pooled origin/destination split pattern. The local pattern, pooled pattern and party-only rule are conceptually distinct: the first preserves local heterogeneity, the second tests whether local detail adds value, and the third tests whether historical splitting adds value at all. No approach is chosen by the holdout result. Three chronological holdouts use only prior-election source kernels. Errors are bounded over published rounding intervals and reported in percentage points of total target party ballots; this is **not** the published valid-candidate-vote share denominator.

For each candidate, the output also retains a full candidate-vote interval: predicted matched votes with rounding bounds plus between zero and all unmatched target party-ballot mass. The observed exact candidate total may be compared with this interval, but the interval cannot be collapsed to a point forecast. A Stage5 valid-party-share-input sensitivity may be calculated using target observed valid-party turnout as a scale; informal-party behaviour and candidate denominator still remain unresolved, so it is not a pre-election forecast. Operational selection requires a complete mapping and stable benchmark improvement, otherwise the selected view remains null. This is frozen before any split prediction/score generation.

For each target candidate, target local party ballot sizes are conditional inputs. Target split cells and exact candidate totals are scoring evidence only. Two-decimal percentages propagate as closed rounding intervals. The local source pattern improves **the matched component** over both benchmarks on each holdout, even comparing its conservative upper error bound with their lower bounds. That is information in the local split tables; it does not imply a deployable candidate forecast. Median unallocated target party-ballot mass is 2.96%, 2.45% and 1.76% by holdout. Exact published candidate totals fall inside the deliberately broad full-vote bounds for only 84/247, 96/285 and 87/271 candidates. Transport error, not merely published rounding, is material. Joint accounting retains matched target-candidate mass, source destinations without a matching target candidate, informal/party-only destinations and unmatched target party ballots separately; no category is renormalized or assigned to a convenient candidate.

No candidate is labelled a confirmed continuation solely because the source and target names match. Stage10's retrospectively acquired status overlaps the scored sample for 39 supported continuations, five supported replacements and four by-election successors; these status groups were **not** used to admit predictions. Another 504 scored pairs are Stage10 unresolved identity, and 242 are retrospective/ambiguous continuations. Supported replacement evidence is selected and too sparse for a transferable replacement-specific split rule. Among the directly observable name groups, local matched-component MAE does not show a consistent continuation advantage: 2011 probable exact chain 2.360–2.380 pp versus different-name unresolved 2.167–2.186; 2017 2.315–2.334 versus 2.485–2.505; 2023 3.107–3.126 versus 3.168–3.188. These are descriptive composition checks, not identity adjudication.

The separately frozen Stage5-input sensitivity substitutes additive or proportional **predicted local valid-party shares** for observed valid-party group sizes on the same 247/285/271 candidates. Additive MAE bounds are 2.370–2.389, 2.682–2.701 and 3.420–3.440 pp, respectively; proportional bounds are 2.470–2.489, 2.962–2.982 and 3.659–3.679 pp. The comparison still uses the target election's *observed national party shares* in the retained Stage5 transform and observed target valid-party turnout as a scale. This timing limitation was made explicit after inspecting the scored sensitivity; it is an interpretation clarification, not a changed kernel, cohort or metric. Informal-party counts, unmatched groups, candidate-denominator and turnout forecasts are missing. These scores are **not** a complete pre-election forecast and do not select a Stage5 transform.

The computational outcome audit toggles all historical candidate winner flags and clears winner IDs while keeping candidacies and official split sources fixed. Applicability does not change. The source corpus was acquired for full election ingestion rather than selected candidate biographies, but this flag test does not establish that the future availability of candidates, parties or split tables is unbiased. There are only three comparable general transition clusters, repeated seats and shared source-year patterns; candidate-event iid significance or individual voter transitions are not inferred.

**Operational selection:** `selectedOperationalSplitView = null`. The benchmark-improvement gate passes on the matched component, but complete party-ballot mass mapping and verified candidate-level transfer do not. This is unresolved applicability, not a measured zero split-ticket effect. Stage7–10 descriptive effects are not stacked with this prediction. A future complete split view would need a rule for party entry, absent target candidate destinations and informal-party/candidate denominator behaviour, validated on whole candidate outcomes. It also needs a target-boundary candidate baseline before 2026 use.

## Evidence for the later design checkpoint

Systematic identity repair across a predetermined cohort could distinguish genuine continuations from replacements and test whether party-level transfer varies with candidate status. It cannot by itself create source patterns for entrant party-ballot groups or a target-boundary baseline. The local pattern is worth retaining as a **conditional component diagnostic** because it beats two simple benchmarks on matched ballots; neither it nor the Stage6 response is yet a separately validated complete predictive view. Before combining views, evaluate complete historical candidate predictions against simple baselines with common holdouts and coverage, repair identity using one protocol for winners and losers, review Stage6 conditional elasticity, and establish candidate baselines on target boundaries. Those are separately authorized tasks, not Stage11 implementation.
