# Stage 9 — freshman-incumbency evidence and analysis

## Pre-fit evidence checkpoint (2026-09-23)

This section and the pinned `data/processed/models/freshman-incumbency/inventory.json` were committed **before fitting**. No effect result was examined to choose these categories. Stage 8's person links and occurrence IDs are retained; the dated tenure records are a separate evidence overlay, not a reclassification of Stage 8's historical outputs.

The acquisition was bounded to 107 New Zealand Parliament individual profiles reachable from source-winner recontesters in the unchanged-boundary Stage 8 pairs, plus the missing second page of the former-member index. The 108 raw files and their exact bytes are registered in `data/source-plans/freshman-incumbency-tenure-sources.json`. Of 107 profiles, 101 have a complete parsed dated service table and six provide no usable dated rows. A profile has its own publication date and 2026 retrieval timestamp; service start/end dates are historical facts, not evidence publication dates. Publication is generally retrospective relative to the election being evaluated.

An observed source-election win, unique official profile with the same surname and explicit service for that electorate on that election date, and a Stage 8 linked target candidacy support the source occurrence. Given names can be shortened on profiles, so name alone never establishes the link. Three source winners have no unique usable dated source-service match. The profile's dated electorate and list rows and first-election date must reconcile before classifying a first-ever win. Complete-profile inference is still conditional on accuracy and completeness of Parliament's retrospective table. The target occurrence may remain only **probable** under Stage 8's exact chain; an identity match does not establish complete career history. Party-label disagreements are recorded without changing the preserved labels. No target-election win or residual is used by the Stage 9 tenure classifier. A **post-fit audit below** found target-outcome dependence in three inherited Stage 8 pair links, so the original inventory's93 primary-eligible records are a historical pre-fit cohort, not the final evaluation cohort.

The inventory contains all 532 adjacent linked Stage 8 pairs and explicit reasons for excluding non-comparable geography or source candidates who did not win. Its first potentially analyzable subset is conditional on a **source winner who recontests** the same electorate across one of three unchanged-boundary transitions. This is not a sample of all incumbents, retirees, or all returning candidates. The 2008 panel edge is handled through dated pre-panel profile rows; first dataset appearance is never treated as first service. The primary treatment requires a reconciled first-ever electorate service start on the source general-election date, no prior list service, and continuing service through target-election day. The comparator has earlier continuing electorate service. A prior electorate MP switching seats at source, a returning former MP, an off-cycle entrant, interrupted service, incomplete tenure, and a prior-list freshman are held outside the primary cohort and retained with reasons for diagnostics or sensitivity. Off-cycle electorate starts in the observed panel are consistent with by-election entry but the table alone does not label an election; this remains an explicit sensitivity group. Prior list service never counts as prior electorate service.

## Frozen statistical specification (before fitting)

The machine-readable specification is `data/processed/models/freshman-incumbency/specification.json`, committed after the inventory checkpoint and before effect fitting. The question is whether **first re-election status predicts later normalized overperformance beyond a prior residual and an experienced-incumbent baseline**. The estimand is a predictive incremental association among source-winner recontesters with documented continuous tenure; it is neither a causal freshman effect nor uniquely personal-vote retention.

On the primary additive percentage-point scale, fit equal-weight ordinary least squares with an intercept: `target residual = α + β × source residual + γ × first-term indicator`. The no-freshman comparator fits `α₀ + β₀ × source residual` to the same training and evaluation pairs. A carry-forward prior residual and zero residual are simple secondary benchmarks. The intercept allows the next-election reference distribution and recontester selection to shift. Stage 6's zero-intercept response addressed a different question. The Stage 9 β is jointly estimated solely to assess γ; it does not replace Stage 8's null operational persistence choice.

An average freshman residual omits baseline differences. A first-term versus experienced difference in before/after changes fixes β at one and can reflect regression to the mean. We retain that change contrast as a sensitivity, but use the one-indicator common-slope model as the smallest adjustment for prior residual and an incumbent comparison. Fixed seat or future-transition intercepts would be unsupported by three transition clusters and unavailable in a genuine holdout. National party changes and stable seat context remain limitations.

For the 2017 holdout, train only on 2011 targets; for 2023, train only on 2011 and 2017 targets. The 2011 transition has no earlier fitting data and receives descriptive/benchmark scores only. Each holdout evaluates full and no-freshman predictions plus simple benchmarks on identical eligible pairs. Source residual, documented pre-result tenure and known target candidacy are predictors/eligibility; target residual/reference offset and target/later wins are evaluation outcomes only. MAE/RMSE use percentage points. Target winners and losers follow identical eligibility rules. This is retrospective conditional-on-recontesting evaluation, not a fully prospective forecast of who stands again.

Predeclared operational gates require treatment and comparator in both trained holdouts, stable effect direction, non-worse MAE and RMSE versus no-freshman in each, ≥0.25 percentage-point pooled MAE improvement, no sign reversal under alternative normalization/prior-list sensitivity, and minimum target-identity breadth: at least one independently confirmed target loser as well as winners in each trained holdout. The last criterion is only a minimum coverage check for general-electorate incumbents, not a claim of representativeness. Otherwise the selected operational effect is null. Proportional/log-odds, prior-list inclusion, off-cycle entrant inclusion and mean-change contrast are reported separately. A both-confirmed Stage 8 identity restriction is **winner-selected retrospective diagnostics** because target confirmation can depend on winning; it cannot govern operational selection. With only three election clusters, repeated candidates and shared party/election reference offsets, no candidate-pair iid standard errors or causal claims are warranted. The explicit target-loser coverage rule was clarified after `fb0f491`, still before fitting; the original specification commit remains in history.

## Post-fit cohort audit and correction

The first fit used the committed93-pair inventory as specified. An audit then separated the **observed target win rate** from actual selection mechanisms. Source-election victory is required by design, and the analysis conditions on the person recontesting. Retrieving retrospective Parliament profiles for this source-winner set can favour MPs with longer or more prominent careers, but the preserved sources do not identify the magnitude of that selection. The observed 85/93 target wins do **not** by themselves show that target victories decided eligibility.

The inherited Stage 8 links were rebuilt in memory for each transition after setting target and every later winner flag to false while leaving candidate records, prior winner flags, official index pages and dated tenure evidence unchanged. The source-winner comparable set falls from125 to122 and the original primary set from93 to90. Three cross-name links disappear: 2008 Hamilton East, 2008 Maungakiekie and 2014 Northcote. These pair links depended on target/later winning anchors, although their Stage 9 tenure classifications did not. No new comparable pairs appear. One profile retrieved for the removed links is not used by any surviving pair; the other two also serve surviving links. An exact-chain pair with a pre-source winner anchor remains in the corrected set. `cohort-audit.json` records every counterfactual pair result and `postfit-audit-amendment.json` preserves the original rule, defect and correction. This amendment was made **after** fitting; the original specification and pre-fit inventory commits remain unchanged. The correction filters all primary and sensitivity analyses and does not tune the model or thresholds.

The final evaluation cohort has90 general pairs:28 in2008→2011 (10 first-term,18 experienced),36 in2014→2017 (9,27) and26 in2020→2023 (12,14). There are no classified Māori source-winner pairs. Target outcomes are observed in this cohort:82 winners and eight losers (27/1,35/1,20/6 by holdout). That high rate is consistent with selection on prior victory and recontesting; it is not proof of target-win-based admission after the three demonstrable links were removed. None of the eight target losers has an independently confirmed Stage 8 occurrence link; they remain probable exact-chain targets. The both-confirmed restriction has25 pairs (eight first-term,17 experienced) and is winner-selected retrospective diagnostics only.

## Corrected results

The full90-pair **descriptive** additive fit is intercept +0.780pp, prior-residual slope0.745 and incremental first-term association **+2.630pp**. A difference in average before/after changes is +3.307pp, which fixes prior-residual persistence implicitly to one and is not the primary estimator. These values describe selected recontesters; they are not causal freshman effects or deployable bonuses. Leaving out one transition at a time gives descriptive first-term terms of +1.842 to +3.365pp, with only three independent election transitions.

The freshman term improves the no-freshman model in2017, then worsens both MAE and RMSE in2023. Pooled MAE improvement is only0.008pp, below the frozen0.25pp gate. The descriptive first-term term stays positive under proportional (+2.374pp), log-odds (+2.539pp), including nine prior-list freshmen (+3.086pp; n99) and including four off-cycle experienced entrants (+2.647pp; n94). These are sensitivities, not independent coefficients. The four off-cycle service starts are consistent with by-election entry; profile tables record dates without naming the electoral event, and all four remain outside the frozen primary comparison. The corrected both-confirmed retrospective fit is+2.307pp on25 winner-selected pairs and cannot validate target losers.

`selectedOperationalFreshmanEffectPP = null`. The 2023 full-model scores worsen versus the no-freshman benchmark, pooled gain is below the frozen materiality threshold, and the target-identity breadth gate fails in both trained holdouts. The null conclusion also respects source-winner/recontesting selection, retrospective profile availability, probable target links, stable seat and party conditions, regression to the mean and shared reference offsets. No Stage8 persistence coefficient is adopted; no freshman increment is added to Stage6/7/8 or transported to changed boundaries. Career evidence for other candidates and replacement effects remain later work requiring separate authorization.
