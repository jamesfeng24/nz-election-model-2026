## What was constructed

All **213** validated unchanged-boundary seat transitions remain in the coverage frame. There are **191 held general contests** with source local split evidence and observed target party-ballot/turnout controls: 63 in 2011, 64 in 2017, 64 in 2023, containing **423/431/459 candidate occurrences** respectively (1,313 total). The 21 Māori contests and cancelled 2023 Port Waikato candidate contest remain coverage-only with distinct reasons. Every standing candidate, including losers, independents and entrants, and three noncandidate destinations shares one ballot ledger. Published target candidate votes, valid-candidate totals, winner flags and target split destinations are absent from construction.

The source-election pool uses all complete held general local matrices, with exact positive origin-row counts as fixed weights. It includes **1,258/1,021/1,169** positive source rows for 2008/2014/2020 and excludes only **2/3/1** zero-mass origins. No positive source row needed exclusion. A complete source seat without a candidate of destination party `h` contributes structural zero to `h` while retaining its origin weight; printed `0.00` is a rounded cell with a nonzero possible upper endpoint. Cell bounds, not rounded party aggregates, enter a jointly conserving row simplex. All 191 target party controls reconcile: named valid-party groups plus informal party and disallowed/unrepresented party ballots equal votes cast. Party-ballot and candidate-ballot votes cast agree, but the latter was audited separately and never used as construction input.

The **primary** target route set is the exact count-weighted linear image of the source row simplexes under the frozen pooled-equality transport assumption. The **separate** source-seat sensitivity uses their convex hull; it does not replace the primary set with independent marginal boxes. Party diagonal and unrestricted ledgers are the frozen comparators. Unsupported/independent target destinations release a whole origin row; absent source destinations release only their coupled category mass. Unknown personal identity has no effect on routing. The party-disallowed origin remains explicit and can contribute candidate-only votes. Each contest conserves its supplied votes cast exactly. None of these sets is a calibrated prediction interval or guaranteed future bound.

## Conditional results on the identical held-general frame

Candidate-vote inclusion counts are **marginal**, one candidate at a time. Joint-vector feasibility asks whether one common ballot allocation could yield **all** observed candidate totals in the contest. Shares use the **valid candidate** denominator; a feasible zero denominator makes the share undefined. Every method was evaluated on the same 63/64/64 contests and 423/431/459 candidate events. The defined-share common subset for primary, heterogeneity and party diagonal contains 49/124/14 candidates; the unrestricted ledger has no defined share.

| 2011 | 54/63 | 49/423 | 402/423 | 54/63 | 63/63 | 1/63 |
| 2017 | 42/64 | 124/431 | 364/431 | 42/64 | 61/64 | 1/64 |

The primary set is narrow when it constrains: **all 34** partly constrained contests reject the complete observed candidate vector (9/22/3 by holdout). This is an empirical failure of pooled-equality transport, not a reason to widen it after inspection. The source-seat hull contains every individual observed candidate total but fails the **joint** observed vector in three 2017 contests: Epsom, Helensville and Northland. A second HiGHS algorithm independently returned infeasible for these three. Marginal inclusion alone therefore overstates what the coupled ballot ledger explains. The party-diagonal simplification is much sharper but jointly feasible in only 1/1/3 contests; the unrestricted ledger is jointly feasible in every contest by construction and contributes no discriminatory information.

Primary candidate shares are undefined for **374/423**, **307/431** and **445/459** candidate events. The unrestricted shares are undefined for all 1,313. On the prespecified common defined-share subset, primary actual inclusion is **29/49**, **56/124**, **9/14**; source-seat hull is **49/49**, **124/124**, **14/14**; party diagonal is **26/49**, **66/124**, **8/14**. The corresponding mean widths are 8.64/6.80/6.59 percentage points for primary, 62.66/49.38/46.04 for the hull, and 10.43/5.56/7.93 for the diagonal. These widths and inclusion rates do not establish calibration. No frozen method produces a candidate-vote point on the common frame, so point MAE/RMSE/bias are **not calculated**. The observed candidate denominator falls inside the primary bounds in 63/60/64 contests, another sign of the narrow transport assumption in 2017.

Outcome breakdowns remain in [diagnostics.json](../data/processed/models/conditional-candidate-ledger/diagnostics.json). The primary informative counts include 9 winners/40 losers in 2011, 22/102 in 2017 and 3/11 in 2023. All winners and losers were admitted by the same pre-result party/category and geography rules; winner flags are evaluation-only. Exact status as continuing/replacement is **not inferred** where person evidence is unresolved.

## Interpretation and limits

Most target contests have a candidate whose destination category is unsupported by earlier source rows or is independent. Under the reviewed conservative rule this releases every positive origin, so a wide containing interval often says only that ballot mass is conserved. The smaller constrained subset is **not** a representative sample of 2026 contests. Primary failure is consistent with source-to-target voting-pattern change, entrant/party mix, independent strength and other unmodelled local conditions; it is not evidence of a personal-vote effect. The convex hull describes **historical source-seat variation**, not future coverage. Stage 11's matched-component information does not by itself repair this complete allocation problem.

This exercise conditions on **observed target local party votes, party informal/disallowed counts, votes cast and retrospective official candidacy**. It is neither a pre-election forecast nor an operational selection exercise. It lacks dated as-of nomination/party/turnout/validity inputs, a comparable Māori source panel and a 2026 target-boundary candidate baseline. Candidate identity remains irrelevant to this identity-free baseline, so Stage 13's sparse confirmed links cannot solve the main unsupported-destination problem here. Operational candidate-ledger selection remains **null**, not an estimated zero effect. The Stage 5 transform, Stage 6 conditional response, Stage 8–10 effects and Stage 11 split view remain unselected/null as previously recorded; none is added as an independent bonus.
