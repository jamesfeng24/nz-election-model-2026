# Stage 25 — reusable historical geography applicability

**Design and evidence checkpoint; no model was fitted or scored.** A [post-checkpoint chronology amendment](stage25-chronology-amendment.md) registers two protocols and specifies practical linkage as the next separately authorized task. The machine-readable [geography](../data/processed/checkpoints/stage25-historical-geography/geography.json), [linked availability](../data/processed/checkpoints/stage25-historical-geography/availability.json), [fold plan](../data/processed/checkpoints/stage25-historical-geography/fold-plan.json), [source composition](../data/processed/checkpoints/stage25-historical-geography/source-composition.json) and [experiment register](../data/processed/checkpoints/stage25-historical-geography/experiment-register.json) are proposed inputs to separately authorized retests. Geography never depends on model results, identity labels or winners. Each model applies its own requirements to linked records. Historical Stages 5–24 and all operational nulls are unchanged.

## Geographic certification and the 29/20 distinction

The canonical record uses election-local electorate IDs, preserves every predecessor edge and keeps target inheritance and source retention as separate rational lower/upper population fractions. It covers all 356 target seats across five adjacent transitions: 213 original same-regime seats, 71 in 2014 and 72 in 2020. It retains all general and Māori seats, including 2023 Port Waikato's cancelled candidate contest. The two redistribution crosswalks and their registered raw/code dependencies are checked against their prior manifests. Source and target labels are recorded but never certify cross-election geography. A preserved 2014 crosswalk label for Rangitīkei is encoding-damaged; its election-local official number and occurrence ID agree, and the label mismatch remains explicit.

|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 2011→2014 | 29 | **20** | 39 / 45 | 28 / 37 |
| 2017→2020 | 34 | **34** | 46 / 49 | 41 / 47 |

The supplied 29/39/45 and 34/46/49 counts reproduce the crosswalk's **one-sided target** statements. In 2011→2014, nine of the 29 incoming-identity targets have a predecessor that also supplies a second target: Epsom, Manukau East, Ōtaki, Rangitata, Rodney, Tāmaki, Waitaki, Wellington Central and Wigram. Those nine cannot be certified as the *same two-sided electorate membership* as their source, despite 100% target inheritance. The primary exact expansion therefore uses 20, agreeing with the preserved 2014 report's 20 unchanged general controls. For 2020, all 34 identity cases have no such outflow. An exact membership set does **not** imply the same residents, voters, turnout or candidate electorate strength at the later election.

Exclusive **two-sided** bands beyond exact are eight ≥95% and nine ≥90% only in 2014, and seven ≥95% and six ≥90% only in 2020; the remaining 27/18 general targets are below the two-sided 90% gate. For Māori, strict two-sided exact counts are five in 2014 and two in 2020; their one-sided identity flags are six and two. Māori geography is present, while comparable named-candidate and split evidence for the specified general models remains unavailable. Approximate bands are inventory and future transport sensitivities only. Each bound is guaranteed on the same certified predecessor edge; no incompatible marginal endpoints, midpoint candidate baselines or candidate-ballot overlap claims are used. Nested-tier agreement would be correlated sensitivity evidence, not independent validation.

The data support an approximate inventory, **not** an approximate candidate forecast rule at this checkpoint.

## Chronological plans, selection and inference population

**Post-checkpoint amendment:** [chronology and linkage contract](stage25-chronology-amendment.md), recorded after original head `3e63f24`, preserves the inherited more-separated protocol as a sensitivity and recommends conventional expanding-window training as primary. Completed training target years may equal the holdout source year, but must precede its target year. The dependency audit distinguishes completed source/training results from target outcomes and unverified historical publication. Sharing an election between a training response and a later predictor induces dependence, not automatically leakage. Historical Stage16/22 outputs and original separated IDs remain unchanged.

Primary baseline/S training counts for 2011/2014/2017/2020/2023 are **0/63/83/147/181**; separated sensitivity counts remain **0/0/63/83/147**. NAT/LAB/source-victory training records are **0/126/166/294/362** versus **0/0/126/166/294**. Evaluation remains 63/20/64/34/64 candidate contests and 126/40/128/68/128 response records. The new **2014 conditional fitted comparison is now chronologically supported**: S coverage and structural contrast gates pass, and NAT/LAB source-win/nonwin count gates pass. Full numerical conditioning remains a later pre-fit prerequisite. 2011 still abstains from fitted transition models. Parameter-free Stage23 and source-election Stage11 constructions do not require earlier transition training.

The two protocols have separate exact fold IDs, training-only preprocessing selectors, original/added IDs and common evaluation sets. Partial-election training uses equal contest/party-seat records, with no implicit equal-election reweighting. Saved-fit transport keeps its actual original parameter/centering contract; it is distinct from expanded refitting. Neither protocol establishes publication by a historical cutoff or a genuine as-of forecast. See the amendment for complete per-family dependencies and limitations.

Ten general seat chains are certified exactly through every adjacent transition. Their IDs are registered as a **stable-seat composition sensitivity**, not a primary or population-representative sample. Existing exact seats are a geographic subset. Source-year descriptive differences already show selection: for 2014 exact versus excluded general targets, mean source National party share is 47.70% versus 50.29%, Labour 28.71% versus 26.31%, and source slate size 6.90 versus 6.61. For 2020, the corresponding values are 46.23% versus 46.96%, Labour 35.87% versus 35.90%, and slate size 6.88 versus 6.48. These are observed **source-election** descriptions, not target outcomes, demographic balance or representativeness weights. The proposed evaluation population is the certified exact-seat subset with each model's additional evidence gates, not all New Zealand electorates or 2026 changed boundaries.

Later reports must show original-common, added and expanded samples; fold-specific MAE/RMSE/signed bias; contest-equal and candidate-equal share metrics where relevant; party/category breakdowns, coverage and abstentions; worst-fold deterioration; and influence diagnostics keeping all primary records. Winner ranking and margin diagnostics apply only to complete-slate candidate shares. Source-victory is a legitimate **source** covariate, not a target-result selection rule. Actual target margin may be retrospective error analysis only. Predeclared source descriptors for such analysis are source party/candidate share, prior gap, source victory, source slate size, geography tier and supported S/V; target residuals, wins and model errors cannot determine applicability. The inherited 0.25 percentage-point threshold remains a development materiality diagnostic, not a significance test or operational approval. These elections have been repeatedly inspected; neither extra seats nor added folds create untouched election environments.

The bounded [experiment register](../data/processed/checkpoints/stage25-historical-geography/experiment-register.json) keeps NAT/LAB response, baseline/S, source-victory, Stage23 party input, Stage11 matched split and deferred V in **distinct outcome and denominator contracts**. Fixed-fit transport, expanded chronological fit and original-training versus expanded-training comparison on identical later evaluation IDs are different designs. The **next proposed implementation**, requiring separate authorization, is the [practical preserved-evidence linkage layer](stage25-chronology-amendment.md#next-separately-authorized-implementation-practical-candidate-linkage): deterministic name/party/context rules with an explicit nickname list, occurrence-level review records and at most 60 preserved-evidence exception inspections. Documentary and accepted algorithmic links remain distinct; failure to match is not replacement and identity is not tenure. No external biography search or linkage occurs in this amendment. Identity-free exact-geography testing is technically independent of this layer and cannot be blocked by a difficult identity exception. The old fixed-fit 2017→2020 transport remains a registered optional design, not the immediate next task. Stage11 remains in its matched-component group, V deferred, and identity-dependent refits await linkage and career-specific gates.

Party geography, S, V, response coefficients and identity effects may all reflect overlapping seat/party conditions. Their correlation would not itself prove double counting, but independent additive stacking has no validated joint error contract. Later comparison should first separate local-party error conditional on observed national support, candidate error conditional on observed local party support, and combined conditional-pipeline error. An as-of replay needs dated national support, nomination and turnout inputs. National reconciliation needs a complete electorate population plus appropriate valid-party weights; the 191 selected candidate contests cannot supply it. Joint uncertainty must propagate upstream national error once, distinguish assumed from calibrated uncertainty, and cover Māori and changed boundaries before any operational integration. A failed mean effect does not establish a variance effect. Stop after a disappointing frozen retest: no automatic variants or broad evidence searches without a specified source route and decision benefit.

## Reproduction and provenance

From the repository root, run `python3 -m scripts.checkpoints.stage25_geography --check`, then `python3 -m scripts.checkpoints.stage25_availability --check`, then `python3 -m scripts.checkpoints.stage25_design --check`. The first revalidates both original boundary manifests and their raw/code dependencies; the second verifies 854 consumed official registry records and raw checksums while tolerating unrelated registry additions; all three compare deterministic output bytes to committed artifacts. Tests are in `scripts/tests/test_stage25_geography.py`, `test_stage25_availability.py` and `test_stage25_design.py`. No raw source, prior numerical artifact, identity adjudication or operational selection was rewritten. All new IDs and experimental rules are proposals awaiting review and separate implementation authorization.
